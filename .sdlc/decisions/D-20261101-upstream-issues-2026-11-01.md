**Two suites moved runner.** slice-ansi 10 and cli-truncate 6.2.0 both moved from ava to `node --test`; with the rows left on ava each control read 0. Both rows now say `node:test`. slice-ansi 10's `tokenize-ansi.js` imports `ansi-styles` 7 and `string-width` instead of `is-fullwidth-code-point`, and its `test.js` imports `chalk` and `strip-ansi`, so `suiteDeps` pins all four at what 10.0.0 resolves. The root manifest moves `slice-ansi` to `^10.0.0`, `cli-truncate` to `^6.2.0` and `ora` to `10.0.0`, and `benchmarks/package.json` pins `ora` and `slice-ansi` at 10.0.0, so each control is the graded release.

**ora 10.0.0.** The suite grew from 99 cases to 223. ora 10 replaced the line count with a layout of the frame cell by cell as a terminal shows it, kept the frame above the last row with a truncation message that shortens to fit, never lets a resize raise the erase count, pauses rendering for an unfinished external line until its newline (ora 9 resumed after 200 ms and drew through it), stops the spinner instead of throwing when a timer, drain or external-write render fails, and changed what is persisted (a plain symbol on a stream that cannot render colour, a new row after an unfinished line, the indent kept). `flagstaff/ora` was re-ported from ora 10's `index.js` method for method over the same family pieces (`roundel/chalk`, `closeout`, `linegauge`'s `width` and `strip`, `paratext/csi`); its write-hook chain stays the `save-restore.qnt` design, which leaves `write` as ora 10's map does once every spinner stops. Eight unit cases in `ora.test.ts` state the behaviours on the façade, and each fails on the ora 9 port. Before the port the target run never finished: `stream with zero columns falls back to 80 columns` hands the ora 9 port `columns: 0`, its line count came out `Infinity`, and the erase loop ran forever, so the oracle's 300 s suite timeout recorded nothing. Counting that case as failed, the ora 9 port read 130 / 223.

**slice-ansi 10.0.0.** A cluster's columns are string-width's now (`counts CRLF as zero width`, `counts columns the same way as string-width`). `linegauge/slice` had followed slice-ansi 9 with a `positions()` rule — every cluster at least one, a lone regional indicator two — and that rule is gone: `slice` cuts by `clusterColumns`, the numbers `width` already gave. That made one branch of `truncate`'s search dead, and the coverage gate saw it: a tail cut from column `total - keep` now always fits, so the tail search could not move off its first guess, and `fit` returns that cut directly. The head search stays, for a zero-width cluster at the boundary.

**cli-truncate 6.2.0.** `preferTruncationOnSpace` finds its space by terminal column over the visible text, and the text after the mark drops its leading spaces instead of being `trim()`med. `linegauge/cli-truncate` builds the same column map over `strip` and the shared segmenter; its differential case over the installed 6.2.0 grows from 1,980 inputs to 2,970. Its `afterSpaces` cut also stopped a start cut from opening on a non-SGR escape, which was the only input that reached `leadingSgrEnd`'s early exit. A colon-form truecolour opener reaches it now, in a case held to the incumbent and to a literal. That case uses no `preferTruncationOnSpace`, because the incumbent strips with Node's `stripVTControlCharacters`, which mis-strips the colon form before Node 24.21.

**ansi-regex 6.5.0 (2026-10-29) is what ten issues were.** boxen, cli-truncate 6.1.1, ink, log-update, ora 9.4.1, string-width, strip-ansi, widest-line, wrap-ansi and yargs each read as a `none` release that grew by exactly 853 B: ansi-regex 6.4.0 -> 6.5.0 in every tree that reaches strip-ansi. 6.5.0 is a behaviour change, not only bytes: a CSI's final byte is never a digit, so `ESC[5d` is no longer cut after the `5`, and a private-parameter CSI (`ESC[>0;276;0c`) is stripped whole. A fresh strip-ansi 7.2.0 install resolves it, so `linegauge/strip` follows it — red first, five shapes held to the incumbent and to literals — and the lockfile's ansi-regex moves to 6.5.0 so `strip.test.ts` compares against the release users get. Node's `stripVTControlCharacters` now differs from strip-ansi on an unterminated `ESC[31` as well as on the colon form.

**Fingerprints, held to the re-graded and re-measured only** (D-20261026-fingerprint-missing-not-rerecord). **Weight.** `flagstaff/ora` walks 44,118 B (+3,749 on the 40,369 recorded), and its budget moves 43,000 -> 44,150, the measurement rounded up to the next 50; ora's own `index.js` grew 8,386 B for the same behaviour. With `roundel/chalk` (9,368 B) it is 53,486 B in two packages against ora 10.0.0's 128,182 B in seventeen, 42% (it was 49% of ora 9.4.1's). linegauge's `cli-truncate.js` ceiling moves 35,239 -> 35,641 (+402) and `widest-line.js` 11,853 -> 11,902 (+49, the strip pattern, no headroom). The `linegauge/cli-truncate` drop-in ceiling in `subpath-weight-lock` is re-measured, not copied: the same method first reproduced 6.1.1's 20,675 B, then gave **21,489 B** for 6.2.0 with slice-ansi 10.0.0 and ansi-regex 6.5.0. The strip pattern adds 49 B to every bundle that measures text; the one ratchet it crossed is `burgee/yargs/v17`'s over-incumbent row, 111,669 -> 111,718.

**What migrate serves.** `SUPPORTED_MAJORS` moves ora to `[10]` and slice-ansi to `[10]`. Neither 9 is graded as a previous major: ora 10 removed cases ora 9's suite asserts (a frame that exactly fits the screen is now truncated, a partial line no longer resumes after a timeout), and slice-ansi 10 answers CRLF and a lone regional indicator the other way from 9, so neither drop-in can be level with the older suite.
