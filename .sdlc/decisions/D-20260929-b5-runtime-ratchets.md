**B5 measures, in-process, how long each entry point takes to do one realistic job against the incumbent it replaces — ours ÷ theirs, the median of per-round ratios over interleaved rounds in a fresh process per pair — and gates every pair with a ceiling in `.sdlc/bands/runtime-ratchets.json` that may only go down.** The target is ≤ 1.0 for every pair. An audit that day measured the opposite on most of them (`paratext/terminal-link` 28× terminal-link, `linegauge/slice` 7.4× slice-ansi, `roundel/chalk` 4.2× chalk, `bellpull` `which.sync` 3.3×) with nothing in the suite able to notice, so the gate lands first and each fix PR lowers its pair's ceiling in the same commit as the fix. A pair above 1.0 is held just above its last CI measurement: the largest median of the last `observations` CI runs times `headroom` (1.25, for a shared runner's noise on an in-process ratio), rounded up to 0.05. The first step of each pair was taken on a loaded laptop at 1.5× and is lowered from the PR's own CI run; after that, `npm run ratchets:propose` reads the CI observations on main. It is a ratio for the reason B2's is (#27): absolute nanoseconds are the runner's, and a round's load lands on both halves of its ratio. Every workload proves both sides print the same bytes before anything is timed, and runs in front of a TTY with colour and hyperlinks forced, because on a pipe chalk styles nothing and paratext prints a link's fallback — a ratio between two different jobs. The rules of the file are the D-157 ratchets' (`scripts/ratchet-steps.ts`); raising a ceiling takes a newer decision than the step it raises. The axis is `runtime` in `npm run bench`, part of `cli-benchmarks`, and runs in the cheap CI job on every PR.
