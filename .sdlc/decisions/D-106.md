**No, and the test said so in 180 ms.** The argument for the swap was good on paper: `closeout/exit-hook` is that package's *drop-in path for the `exit-hook` package* (its design R6), published for somebody migrating off that dependency, and reaching for it from inside the family means caique ships the incumbent's semantics and pays **1,850 bundled bytes** for a compatibility layer it is not the consumer of. `closeout/cursor`'s `hideCursor(stream, onExit)` even takes the registrar as a parameter, so the swap was two lines and it typechecked. It also broke `raw.test.ts`: *"SIGINT puts the cursor back, and still ends the process"* expected **130** and got `null`, and the SIGTERM case expected **143** and got `null`. The façade **re-raises the signal after the handlers run**, so the process leaves with 128 + n the way a signalled process must; `onExit` on its own restores the cursor and then does not. That is the 1,850 bytes, and they are not overhead — they are E5, and caique's own suite is what proves it. Reverted. Recorded so the next reading of this file does not re-derive the same two-line change: the rule is that a façade may be the right internal dependency when what it wraps is a *behaviour* and not just an API, and the way to find out is to make the change and run the suite
