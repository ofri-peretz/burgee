**A B5 ceiling is now the cold-start claim's rule applied to the pair's own CI series: over every bench run (every attempt) whose commit carries the pair's current code, the larger of mean + 3 sample standard deviations and the largest reading, rounded up to 0.05 — recorded beside each ceiling in `derive.rule`.** The first ceilings were 1.25× one run, several of them 1.25× a laptop's, and the spread across GitHub's runners is wider than that: the same commit reads `linegauge/slice` 0.787 on one CPU and 1.028 on another, so a 0.95 ceiling red-lit PRs that never touched linegauge (#774, green on its third attempt). Where a pair's code changed so recently that its own series has fewer than eight runs (`roundel/chalk`, `flagstaff/ora` and `flagstaff/cli-table3` after #770; `bellpull/node-which` after #766), its σ is taken relative to its mean from the longest series of the same workload, because the runner's spread is a property of the workload and the machine, not of the commit. Two ceilings rise under this rule — `linegauge/slice` 0.95 → 1.05 (max 1.028) and `flagstaff/log-update` 0.95 → 1.05 (max 0.998) — and twelve fall. **A ceiling a spec or a decision writes down is not a ratchet from spread and is never raised**: `bellpull ÷ tinyexec` stays at bellpull R8's 1.0 (D-20260930-bellpull-spawn-vs-tinyexec) and its noise is cut with 31 rounds instead of 11; `paratext`, `linegauge/slice` and `flagstaff/log-update` take 21, which narrows the in-run median without moving a ceiling. The downward-only rule is unchanged: a later step may only lower a ceiling, or raise it citing a decision newer than this one. `npm run ratchets:propose` reads the committed CI observations with the same rule.
